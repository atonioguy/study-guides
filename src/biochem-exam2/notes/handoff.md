## Why this build is different from Exam 1
The real Exam 1 was surface-level and lecture-heavy: unlabeled figures with an arrow or a blank, graphs, questions on the lecture's own examples and numbers, and T/F in the lecture's wording. The Exam 1 guide lessons worked. The practice questions did not: too few, too conceptual, built from asides, and they read like more studying instead of like the exam.
So: the lecture drives everything. LOs are a loose label, not the source of questions. Don't teach more or less than the lecture does. The job of the question bank is to rehearse the exam.

## Voice (Antonio was specific about this)
The site must read like something the course's professor made. Nothing on the page may hint at the instructions behind it.
- No labels or copy that describe the build: no "exam-style", "specific example", "core", "inventory", "as worded in lecture", "this tests whether you can". `kind` and `inv` are internal fields and are never shown.
- Explanations state the fact and stop. One or two sentences. No coaching, no "remember that", no commentary on why the question was asked.
- Lessons: plain TA-notes voice, short, a figure where it helps. No em dashes, no "isn't X, it's Y", no tagline headings, no triplets, no hype.
- Wording corrections go in a short note that reads like a professor's footnote, only where a student would otherwise be confused. Don't flag every typo.
- Don't over-apply any one instruction. If a rule is making the text sound like it is following a rule, back off.

## Reference set (Nov 17): M4 Lesson 2 is the pattern to match
`m4.js` holds the six L2 lessons (`m4-map` to `m4-reg1`); questions `m4b01` to `m4b16` are the reference questions. Write every other lesson and question the same way, then add to the same file.
- Lessons: id and heading from `inventory.js`, lecture image through `img` (credit "Lecture figure, Lesson N."), the lecture's own wording for facts, a table or list only where the lecture compares things, one footnote-style note where the lecture contradicts itself, a one-line `so`. Tables stay at three columns or fewer and step lists are `<ol>`, because wider tables clip at 390 px. Long single words in a narrow first column get `&shy;` (m4-class).
- Questions: id is `m4` + lecture letter (a = L1 ... e = L5) + number. Each has `kind`, `inv`, an `HL` snippet from its lesson and a `LEC` block + verbatim lecture snippet. Stems ask for the lecture's fact directly. Explanations are one or two sentences. About 3 T/F per lesson's worth, mostly false. In a "which belongs in the blank" figure, the distractors must not be molecules already printed elsewhere in the figure.
- Figures: `gen_figs.py` writes `figs.js`. `ladder()` draws any run of glycolysis steps with enzymes, cofactors, numbers, lettered steps or "?" blanks switched by arguments, so L3 figures are new calls, not new code. `q_pfk()` takes the regulator lists the same way (a number makes a blank). `q_phases()` is the two-phase summary (rows, ATP labels and phase labels; a number makes a blank). `fischer()`/`q_sugars()` draw linear sugars from `SUG` (add a sugar there), numbered or lettered, `mirror=` for a mirror image. `q_disacc()` and `q_lact()` are L1. `q_reg()` is the general regulator map behind `q_pfk()` and `q_pk()`; `q_fork()`, `q_pdh()` and `q_nadloop()` are L3 (a number makes a blank). L4: `q_glybond()`, `q_horm()`, `q_gsrc()` (the glucose-source graph, curves or states as names, letters or numbers; L5 reuses it), `q_tissue()` (liver vs muscle breakdown; "|" breaks an enzyme name), and `q_pdh()` now takes a number in `rows` and `None` in `side` (glycogenesis figures). L5: `q_loop()` (lactate from muscle to liver), `q_bypass()` (glycolysis down, gluconeogenesis up, numbered bypass enzymes, lettered shared stretch); `q_reg()` skips its legend when a list is empty (`q-pepck`). Reuse a figure for more than one question, and never number a blank the same as a step number already in the figure.

## Also in v1.4: tab scroll spots survive a trackpad flick (Nov 23; pushed)
Antonio: switching between practice and the study guide loses his spot on both. Per-tab spots were already saved (`S.pos`, `savePos()`), and a plain switch restored them. What broke them: clicking a tab while a trackpad flick is still coasting. The remaining wheel events land on the tab that just opened and scroll it off its spot, and then that drifted spot gets saved. Going back and forth drifts both tabs.
- Fix in `go()`: if a wheel event arrived in the 150 ms before the click, `holdCoast()` pins the page after the restore (`overflow:hidden` on html, `scrollbar-gutter:stable` so nothing reflows) until wheel events stop for 150 ms (3 s cap), or right away on a key, touch or pointer press. `savePos()` skips while pinned, and `go()` clears the pending debounced save. A normal click (no wheel just before it) does nothing different.
- `test_site.py` has `tab_spots()` (own context, both widths): plain round trip, round trip mid-flick for both tabs, page unpinned afterwards, fresh scroll still works. 298 ok, 0 failures.
- Not covered: touch momentum on a phone (iOS stops momentum on a tap, so it shouldn't carry over), and WebKit couldn't be tested here (Playwright's WebKit download is blocked).

## Lecture wording problems (answer keys follow the lecture; write questions so both readings give the same answer)
M4: glucose, fructose and galactose said to differ in carbon number (L1 b22; all are hexoses). hexokinase "higher Km (affinity)" (Km is inverse of affinity; ask about affinity or the graph, never "which has the higher Km"). 32 vs 36 ATP aerobic (don't ask for the number; 2 ATP anaerobic is safe). DHAP called an aldotriose. Pyruvate kinase placed in the mitochondria. Pyruvate decarboxylation said to consume an oxygen molecule. "Activity of lactate" for lactate dehydrogenase. Phosphoglucomutase called the key insulin-activated enzyme while the check says glycogen synthase is rate-limiting (use glycogen synthase). Glycogenin called a short chain of glycogen. Muscle said to be unable to convert G1P to G6P (the missing enzyme is glucose-6-phosphatase; ask what muscle lacks and that it can't release glucose). Liver said to make glucose from fatty acids in starvation (L5 list is amino acids, lactate, glycerol). Skeletal muscle listed as able to do gluconeogenesis. "ATP must be very low to activate" gluconeogenesis (avoid).
M5: trans fats "higher melting point than saturated" (ask solid vs liquid, man-made, not a melting-point ranking against saturated). Glycolipids called a membrane protein. Ceramide called the fatty acid. "FAD+", "FADH", "enol-CoA hydratase", "thiloase". Thiolysis "adds a new acyl-CoA". Fatty acids entering through "voltage dependent ion channels" (the lecture's own check says they do not enter the matrix that way). "Enzymes such as prostaglandins introduce double bonds". Phospholipids "AKA phosphoglycolipids". "SREBP is inhibited by insulin" (test only the direction: insulin and thyroid hormone raise synthesis, glucagon and glucocorticoids lower it).
